Scenarios — research, decisions, test

The Scenarios tab (after My Home): simple automations an ordinary family understands at a glance, that still work reliably. What other apps do, what goes wrong for people, what we chose, and how the prototype did in a task test.

01What the big apps do

Every app has the same three parts — something starts it, optional checks, then actions — but they name and split them differently, and that is where people get lost.

02What research says about people making rules

Beginners can do it

In a 226-person test, people with no programming background quickly made rules with several starts and actions. “If this, then that” works as a model.

Ur et al., CHI 2014
But half the rules had bugs

In a 153-person study, half of the rules people wrote did not do what they meant — mostly timing (“when” vs “while”), order, and wrong expectations.

Trace2TAP study
Conditions and time are the hard part

Non-programmers struggled most with condition blocks, time-related tasks and debugging — not with picking devices.

Block-based smart-home study, 2021
Fixing is harder than making

Across studies, finding out why a rule misbehaved costs more than writing it. People need to see what happened.

McCall et al., SOUPS 2023, and the studies it cites

03What we chose — and why

One tab, two kinds, everyday words

“Tap to run” (tiles, like device tiles) and “Automatic” (cards with a switch) on one page. One editor with a switch between them — no “scene / routine / precondition” vocabulary.

Tuya’s clarity without its two lists
The whole scenario as one sentence

The editor builds and shows it live: “When Front door opens, only at night, turn on Hall for 5 min.” The same sentence is the card on the list. People check logic in words, not in boxes.

Answers the “rules with bugs” problem
Starts when · Only if · Do

Three sections that read top to bottom. “Any one starts it”, “all must be true” are said in a short line under the list — never as an Any/All switch.

Google’s structure, without its logic words
“For X min” inside the action

Turn on → “then turn it off after 2 / 5 / 10 min”. The most common automation (light on motion or door) needs no second rule — the classic source of bugs.

Apple Home
Ideas filled with your own devices

Good night, I’m leaving, Light when I come in at night, Door left open, Laundry is done, Too warm… Only ideas this home can actually run are shown; tap → it opens already complete → Add.

Aqara / Alexa templates, made personal
“What happened” — and why it didn’t

Each card says when it last ran and what started it. A skipped run says why in words: “Skipped — not at night”. The editor keeps the last runs.

Research: debugging is the hard part
Try it now

Runs the actions once so people see what will happen, without waiting for the night or for a door to open.

SmartThings “test”
Safety rules built in

Unlock is only in Tap to run (a door never opens on its own). Two automations that fight over the same light, or one that switches itself back and forth, get a warning before saving. A scenario can’t restart itself within 3 s.

Apple’s unlock confirmation; loop bugs from research
Nothing hidden

Automations that live in device settings (lights on motion, close the water on a leak, smoke actions…) are counted on the Scenarios page and listed in one sheet, so people know they exist.

Our own device pages
Reliable by design

Device starts run on the hub (works when phones are off). Sunrise / sunset move with the seasons by themselves. Location is the only phone-dependent start — and it is labelled so.

Apple’s location trouble after iOS 26

04What is in the prototype

  • Scenarios tab — Tap to run tiles (tap runs it, pen edits), Automatic cards (sentence, last run, switch), Ideas carousel, Built into your devices.
  • Starts: at a time (days), sunrise / sunset ± minutes, a device (door opens / closes / stays open for X min, motion, someone comes in / room empty, water, smoke, gas, lock / unlock, warmer / colder than, air gets worse, washer starts / finishes / is shaken, power above X kW, any light / relay / plug / curtain / valve on or off), people leave / arrive.
  • Only if: at night / in the daytime / between two times, days of the week, a device is in a state.
  • Do: control a device (with “for X min”), send a notification (or an urgent alert), wait, run a scene.
  • Real engine: scenarios really switch the prototype’s devices. Sensor events from each device page’s test panel start them; waits and “for X min” survive a page change.
Try it: open Front door, open the door in the test panel → “Light when I come in” runs at night, or is skipped in the daytime and says why on the Scenarios page.

05Test — tasks run on the real prototype

Honest scope: this is not a test with real people yet. It is (1) a script that does each user task on the prototype the way a person would tap, checks the result in the device model and counts taps, and (2) a walkthrough of the same tasks as three typical users. It finds broken flows and friction; it can’t tell how real people feel. A plan for a real test is in section 07.

Walkthrough as three users

Dilnoza, 34 — wants light when she comes home late

Sees “Light when I come in at night” in Ideas → Add. Done in 2 taps; the sentence tells her exactly what it does. Easy.

Akmal, 58 — “turn everything off when we leave”

Finds “I’m leaving” and adds it — but expects it to happen by itself when the family leaves, while it is a tap scene. Open question: offer “or run it when everyone leaves” right on that idea.

Jasur, 27 — motion light only at night, 2 min

Builds it from zero in 12 taps and checks it with Try it now. Likely friction: the device list is sorted by room in the home’s order, not starting with the room he is in. Works, small polish left.

Fixed during the test

  1. “Built into your devices” was 10 rows long and pushed everything down → one row with a count, the list opens in a sheet.
  2. “When” said twice (“Starts when / When Front door opens”) → the row now reads “Front door opens”.
  3. 14 taps to build from zero → 12: devices are listed in the first sheet, no extra “choose a type” step.
  4. Single-choice sheets had a Done button → they close as soon as you pick (our sheet rule); Done stays only where you set a value (time, minutes).
  5. Channel names alone (“Light”, “Boiler”) were unclear in pickers → the device name is shown under them.
  6. An unfinished scenario read “When …, do nothing yet.” → it now says what to add next.

Still open

  • Sentences use channel names as they are (“turn on Hall”) — clear only if channels are named well.
  • An automation’s own action doesn’t start another automation (stops loops). Some people may expect chains — decide.
  • In the prototype, time starts run while a page is open; on the real hub they always run.
  • Location (leave / arrive) is simulated from the desktop test panel.

06Rules for the real app

  • Words: Scenarios · Tap to run · Automatic · Starts when · Only if · Do. Never: trigger, condition, precondition, routine, any/all.
  • Every scenario is shown as its sentence. If something can’t be said in a short sentence, it doesn’t belong in the simple editor.
  • Every run is logged with its cause; every skip with its reason.
  • Safety: no automatic unlock; urgent alerts only for leaks, smoke, gas, break-ins.

07Next: a real test with 5 people

Five people from the target group (not designers), 20 minutes each, on a phone with the live prototype, thinking aloud:

  1. “Make the hall light come on when you come home at night.” (success = via Ideas or from zero; time; errors)
  2. “Before bed you want everything off and the door locked with one tap.”
  3. “The lamp came on yesterday at noon — find out why it didn’t / did.” (tests the log)
  4. “Get a message when the washing machine finishes.”
  5. “Stop the morning curtains for the holidays, but don’t delete them.”

Measure: done without help, taps, time, and one question after each task — “what will this scenario do?” (they read it back in their own words; it should match the sentence).
